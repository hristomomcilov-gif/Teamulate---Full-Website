A practical buyer guide for founders and lean teams deciding whether to build a traditional department, deploy an AI department, or combine both.

The thesis
Most businesses do not actually need eleven additional payrolls. They need the coordinated capabilities those eleven specialists provide. Teamulate is designed to deliver broad marketing coverage through a coordinated AI agent team that can work from the same priorities, plans and operating context — while keeping humans in control of the decisions that genuinely require human judgment.

1. Executive summary and comparison rule

A fully staffed marketing department is powerful. Salary is the visible cost. The less visible cost includes employer payroll contributions, benefits, recruiting, onboarding, tools, management time, knowledge transfer, coordination, vacation coverage, sick leave, turnover, and the need to keep eleven people synchronized.

National wage benchmarks from August 2026 put an eleven-role structure at approximately US $960,490 in U.S. base annual wages and C$866,486 in annualized Canadian base wages. After employer-cost relationships (BLS ECEC March 2026; Canadian employer CPP/EI plus an illustrative 15% benefits-plan premium), modeled total employer compensation is about US $1,401,956 (~US $116,830/month) and C$1,060,174 (~C$88,348/month). That is before recruiting, equipment, software, office costs, provincial payroll taxes, workers compensation, severance or turnover.

Important comparison rule
This is a comparison of functional coverage, not a claim that one AI agent is a human employee equivalent. Humans and AI are different operating resources. Teamulate is intended to cover specialized marketing functions with coordinated agents, while human leadership remains essential for business judgment, brand taste, sensitive approvals and accountability.

3. Hidden operating cost

  • Time off is coverage you still pay for.
  • Knowledge lives in people and leaves with turnover.
  • Recruiting is a bottleneck, not a weekend task.
  • Coordination — meetings, briefs, handoffs — is a hidden headcount.
The alignment paradox
Disagreement can surface better ideas. AI alignment is an advantage only when the direction is good. If the brief is wrong, aligned agents will be wrong together.

4. What changes with an AI marketing department

Teamulate delivers capabilities through eleven named seats, not eleven chatbots: Strategos, Scout, Wordsmith, Seeker, GrowthTrack, Pixel, Flow, Socialite, Nexus, Metric and Guardian. The mapping to human functions is coverage, not a one-to-one employee swap.

6. Where humans are still better — and where AI is stronger

Humans remain better at strategy that depends on taste, brand judgment outside the guide, relationships, risk calls, and accountability. AI is stronger at research assembly, preparation, production and repetition, monitoring, reporting, documentation and cross-channel execution from one context.

The hybrid principle
Keep humans closest to strategy, taste, relationships, risk and accountability. Keep AI closest to research, preparation, production, repetition, monitoring, reporting, documentation and cross-channel execution.

The practical difference is not “AI works while humans do nothing.” Humans spend less time moving information between specialists and more time deciding what to do next.

7. The risk of perfect alignment

A human team leaks. People disagree, forget, or ignore a brief they do not believe. That waste is real — and so is the accident that a dissent surfaces the better idea. An AI department will not give you that accident for free. Compensation has to be designed in: a human who can reject the strategy; an explicit request for alternative creative directions; Guardian on claims; Metric measuring independently; and a rule that stale context is a stop, not a push-through.

9. What “up to 90% lower” means

The live public people-cost claim is up to 90% lower — modeled, not a guarantee. Software and advertising spend sit outside the percentage on both sides. The baseline in this report is the eleven-role national wage set, then the modeled employer load. Your baseline may be smaller, larger, or different by city. The percentage moves with that baseline and with the Teamulate plan you actually buy.

Public prices, in CAD: Core C$7,500 setup + C$5,000/mo; Growth C$12,500 + C$7,500/mo; Scale C$20,000 + C$12,000/mo. Those are retainers for a coordinated department, not a promise that output will match eleven specialists hour for hour, and not a USD sticker.

Credible positioning
The honest claim is not “AI equals eleven employees.” It is coordinated access to eleven specialties without an eleven-person payroll.

10. Who should choose which model

  • Teamulate when you need broad coverage and will stay in the loop on judgment, but do not want eleven additional payrolls.
  • Hybrid when a human stays closest to strategy, taste and relationships, and the department carries research, production, repetition and monitoring. Hybrid is the default honest shape.
  • Human-team-first when the work is the relationship, the product is highly original creative direction, or the risk profile requires a fully human chain of employment.

11. Buyer questions

Is this a claim that one AI agent equals one employee? No. Functional coverage, not employee-equivalence.

What do the figures describe? An illustrative eleven-role department using BLS and Job Bank national wages, then modeled employer load. Not a quote for your market.

Do I still need a human marketing lead? You still need humans closest to strategy, taste, relationships, risk and accountability.

12. Decision

Build the capability before you build the payroll. Hire the named human when the work has proven it needs one.
